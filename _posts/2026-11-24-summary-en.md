### [LeCun tweet accuses Dario Amodei of hyping AI dangers to make open models illegal](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2124550037912920090) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A tweet attributed to Yann LeCun \(@ylecun\) accuses Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei of “hyping the dangers” of AI so that open-source models would be made illegal and he would stay in control; it drew a large Hacker News thread \(1501 points, 845 comments\) published November 23, 2026. A thread comment reposts what it presents as the full text, which claims the danger narrative has run “since GPT-2,” compares the effect to the Ottoman Empire banning the printing press for Arabic, and calls it “a new form of medieval obscurantism.” No supporting source material accompanies the post, and the claim is an opinionated accusation rather than verified reporting, but the discussion extended to concrete regulatory speculation, including hardware-level restrictions on consumer GPUs for LLM workloads.

hackernews · MrBuddyCasino · Nov 23, 17:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50615308)

**「The open-weights fight behind the tweet」** Yann LeCun, a Turing Award winner and longtime advocate of open AI research who has repeatedly dismissed AI doomsaying, is attacking Dario Amodei, who left OpenAI in 2020 to co-found Anthropic, the lab most associated with calls for tight control and government oversight of frontier AI. LeCun&\#x27;s &quot;since GPT-2&quot; reference points back to Amodei&\#x27;s OpenAI years, when OpenAI&\#x27;s 2019 decision to initially withhold the full GPT-2 model over misuse concerns became an early flashpoint over restricting access to models; the tweet also recasts Amodei&\#x27;s OpenAI departure as being about keeping control rather than safety. The accusation lands amid an unresolved policy debate over whether openly downloadable model weights should face legal restrictions — the very outcome LeCun alleges that safety advocacy from labs like Anthropic is meant to bring about.

**「Concrete stakes for open-weight developers」** Whatever one makes of LeCun&\#x27;s accusation, the regulatory question behind it has direct consequences for people building on open-weight models: an August 2026 policy analysis found no US nationwide ban on open-weight AI yet, but government-device bans and other restrictions are escalating, and the fight already affects which models developers can use, where they can deploy them, and what they cost. Commenters in the thread — most specifically iFred, who described conversations with people working at the intersection of regulation and AI — reported that discussion of restricting open weights centers on hardware rather than model files, from GPU microcode updates that limit consumer cards&\#x27; ability to run LLMs to registration of privately owned compute; if such measures advanced, they would fall hardest on hobbyists and small developers running models on consumer hardware, and hardware-level limits could not be patched around. Organized industry pushback is already visible: on July 24, 2026, twenty-five organizations across the AI stack, including Nvidia, Meta, and Microsoft, signed a coordinated letter arguing open-weight models are central to US AI leadership. Builders whose work depends on open-weight deployments on consumer GPUs have a concrete compatibility stake in how this policy fight resolves and should track the actual proposals rather than the personalities.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters engaged more with the policy mechanics than with verifying the accusation: one user citing conversations with people in AI regulation reported that the emerging idea for how open-weight models could be banned is to “ban the guns” instead of the bullets, with floated measures including GPU microcode updates that limit consumer cards for LLM workloads and registration of privately owned compute. Others pushed competing framings — that labs build dependence through subsidized plans before forcing users onto API pricing, or that frontier labs’ large compute budgets rather than open models pose the real danger — which remain opinionated arguments, not established facts.
