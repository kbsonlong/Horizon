### [PHP: mixed-case httpS:// URLs silently skip TLS; fixed in 8.2.36+](https://x.com/ljachowicz/status/2123477025268924514) ⭐️ 8.0/10

PHP&\#x27;s HTTP and FTP stream wrappers match URL schemes case-insensitively but only enable TLS when the scheme&\#x27;s fifth character is a lowercase &\#x27;s&\#x27;, so URLs like httpS://, HTTPS://, or FTPS:// are sent as plaintext HTTP/FTP — exposing tokens, Basic auth credentials, cookies, POST bodies, and FTP logins, including when a third-party redirect targets such a URL. The reported flaw is tracked as CVE-2026-128064 \(GHSA-f6gg-rchx-xw8j\) with a CVSS 4.0 score of 8.2, and is fixed in PHP 8.2.36, 8.3.37, 8.4.28, 8.5.13, and 8.6.0; PHP 8.1 and earlier are end-of-life and will not be patched, leaving manual lowercasing of URL schemes as the only workaround. The claim originates from a social media post relayed via Telegram, so the CVE identifier and version numbers could not be independently verified here.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 20, 03:04

**「Background」** PHP&\#x27;s built-in http:// and ftp:// stream wrappers let functions such as file\_get\_contents\(\) and fopen\(\) fetch remote URLs, and the URL specification treats schemes as case-insensitive, so HTTPS:// is expected to behave identically to https://. The flaw \(CVE-2026-128064, CVSS 8.2, published November 19, 2026\) exists because the wrappers matched scheme names case-insensitively but enabled TLS only when the character at a fixed position was a lowercase &\#x27;s&\#x27;, silently sending mixed-case URLs in plaintext. The fix shipped as part of PHP&\#x27;s coordinated November 19, 2026 security release, which also bundled other critical fixes such as a PHP-FPM request-smuggling flaw and an RCE tied to the session upload progress feature.

**「Impact」** PHP applications that pass URLs built from user input or third-party redirects to stream functions such as file\_get\_contents\(\) can silently send Basic auth credentials, cookies, tokens, and POST bodies in plaintext whenever the scheme contains an uppercase S, letting an on-path observer read data the application expected TLS to protect; the advisory lists every release before 8.2.36, 8.3.37, 8.4.28, and 8.5.13 as affected. Teams should upgrade to those releases or 8.6.0 \(8.2.36 is already published\), and on EOL PHP 8.1 and earlier the only mitigation is normalizing the scheme to lowercase before issuing the request.

hackernews · Terretta · Nov 19, 16:21 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50562113)

**「Background」** Under the UK&\#x27;s Investigatory Powers Act, the Home Office can serve companies with Technical Capability Notices — orders that can compel access to customers&\#x27; encrypted data and whose existence the government has fought to keep a state secret. Challenges to such surveillance are heard by the Investigatory Powers Tribunal, an independent court that allows citizens to contest government surveillance orders. Before this ruling, media reporting had described a capability notice served on Apple — including a second notice reportedly restricted to British users — but the government had refused to officially confirm that any order existed.

**「UK users lose end-to-end iCloud encryption」** The practical consequence for UK users has already materialized: rather than build the demanded access mechanism, Apple withdrew Advanced Data Protection \(ADP\) from new UK users in February 2025, and existing UK users were eventually required to disable it, ending end-to-end encryption for their iCloud data. The TCN reportedly demanded a capability to access ADP-protected iCloud data without notifying users, so developers and organizations relying on Apple&\#x27;s end-to-end encryption guarantees should treat them as legally overridable in the UK. Because the notice&\#x27;s full contents remain secret, the exact scope of any capability Apple was compelled to build is still unknown.

**「Community discussion」** Commenter natch argues Apple has weakened its security assurances in a way that is arguably deceptive, noting a shift from &quot;nobody can access your data, not even Apple&quot; to &quot;Apple cannot access your data,&quot; and contending that a device-resident key may only be a key-encryption key \(KEK\) protecting the actual data key, which Apple could potentially reach. fweimer points to the tribunal&\#x27;s primary judgement and its finding that the press learned of the notice through confidential briefings rather than a leak, while other commenters frame the UK&\#x27;s mass-surveillance posture as unusually aggressive by Western standards.
